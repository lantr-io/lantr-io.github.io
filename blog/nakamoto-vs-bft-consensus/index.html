Two Consensus Families, One Huge Difference

Alex Nemish — May 7, 2026

Two Consensus Families, One Huge Difference

Why Nakamoto and BFT chains are not comparable on the axis that matters most

There are two consensus families in production today.

Nakamoto consensus (Bitcoin, Cardano) treats the heaviest chain — the one with the most accumulated work or stake behind it — as the canonical truth. To rewrite history or stop the chain, an attacker needs to control 51% of that resource.

Byzantine Fault Tolerant consensus in its classical form (TON, Sui, Algorand) requires 2/3 of validators to agree before a block is final. To halt the chain or censor transactions, an attacker needs only 33%.

Look at how nations vote in the UN. It is practically impossible for any pure BFT chain to be truly decentralized at planetary scale. The threshold gap is not a minor implementation detail — it determines what kind of adversary the system can survive.

Finality

Nakamoto — probabilistic

Reorg probability decays exponentially with depth.

  • Bitcoin: pragmatic finality at ~6 blocks (~1 hour). Coinbase transactions become spendable only after 100 blocks (~17 hours).
  • Cardano: practical finality ~2 minutes. Full settlement at 2160 blocks, which spans roughly 12–36 hours depending on stake distribution and slot luck.

Ethereum — hybrid

Ethereum runs Nakamoto-style fork choice (LMD-GHOST) underneath a BFT finality gadget (Casper FFG). Finality lands roughly 12–15 minutes after a block, and requires 2/3 of staked ETH to attest. Reverting a finalized block burns at least 1/3 of all stake — currently around $30B at typical ETH prices. Past the finality boundary, Ethereum inherits the BFT threshold profile entirely.

Solana — also hybrid, inverted

Optimistic confirmation arrives in ~400ms; full finalization (32 slots, "rooted" commitment) takes ~12.8s and requires 2/3 of stake. Same 33% halt threshold as any BFT chain, with the additional cost of seven major outages since mainnet launch, most lasting hours and requiring a coordinated validator restart. The most recent was February 6, 2024, when an infinite-recompile loop in the program execution path halted the network for ~5 hours.

Pure BFT — TON, Sui, Algorand

Instant finality, no reorgs. Fast. Not decentralized.

Censorship — the threshold cuts both ways

Nakamoto at 33%

You produce roughly 33% of blocks. The other 67% include the censored transaction. Inclusion latency increases by ~1.5x. To actually exclude a transaction permanently you need 51% AND have to keep extending the censoring chain faster than the honest one, indefinitely. The censor pays the full cost of attack and gets degraded service in return. Visible, expensive, ineffective.

BFT at 33%

Pure BFT halts. Ethereum keeps producing blocks but finality stalls until the protocol's inactivity leak burns non-attesters' stake back under the threshold. Clever — but the protocol is now deciding who counts as "absent" vs "censoring" vs "legitimately partitioned." That's policy, not consensus.

The sharper point: BFT enables finality censorship without halting. A third of attesters refusing to finalize blocks containing some specific transaction prevents settlement while everything else proceeds. The transaction appears in non-final blocks but never settles. Nakamoto has no analog — once a block is buried, no minority can un-bury it.

This is not theoretical for Ethereum

After OFAC sanctioned Tornado Cash on August 8, 2022, several major MEV-Boost relays — Flashbots being the largest at the time — began filtering out blocks containing transactions touching sanctioned addresses. Validators connected to those relays would only ever receive OFAC-compliant blocks to sign. They weren't actively censoring; they just never saw the alternative.

By November 21, 2022, 79% of post-Merge blocks were OFAC-compliant, per mevwatch.info. That wasn't 33% of stake. It was market structure on a handful of professional builders and relays — Flashbots, BloXroute, Eden — most of them US-jurisdiction companies that ~90% of validators outsourced block construction to via MEV-Boost.

The protocol-level censorship threshold is 33% of stake. The practical censorship threshold turned out to be control over maybe 3–5 companies. The number has since dropped to roughly 30–40% as non-censoring relays (Ultra Sound, Agnostic) gained share, but the peak demonstrated the structural point: BFT validator sets are smaller, professionalized, and jurisdictionally identifiable.

Solana's censorship surface is worse along a different axis

~1,414 active validators sounds decentralized. But the Nakamoto coefficient — the smallest number of independent operators needed to control 33% of stake — sits around 19–20. The hardware bar (high-performance servers, 1Gbps+ symmetric, low-latency relay connections) keeps the operator set professionalized and concentrated in known datacenters. Four jurisdictions each hold over 10% of stake (US 18.3%, Netherlands 13.7%, UK 13.7%, Germany 13.2%). The 33% threshold sits in very few hands.

Bonded vs liquid stake compounds this

Ethereum and Solana require validators (and indirectly, delegators via liquid staking tokens like Lido and Jito) to lock capital with exit delays and slashing risk. That capital is identifiable, addressable, and creates exit friction — both for delegators trying to leave a misbehaving operator and for the operators themselves. Lido alone is ~24% of all staked ETH, down from a ~32% peak in 2023 specifically because the community organized to push it under the 33% threshold. Switching away from a misbehaving LST means eating the exit queue and gas.

Cardano's stake is liquid: ADA stays in your wallet, delegation is a signed certificate pointing at a stake pool, redelegation is one transaction with zero opportunity cost. There is no locked capital to seize, no exit queue to weaponize, no slashing to insure against. Operator misbehavior costs them delegators in one epoch.

This also shapes the operator set. Bonded chains require serious capital per validator (32 ETH, ~$80k on Ethereum), which pushes toward professionalization. Cardano SPOs run with a 500 ADA pledge — two orders of magnitude lower. That is part of why Cardano sustains over 3,000 active stake pools across far more jurisdictions than any bonded chain, with no single pool exceeding ~1.4% of stake.

Honest tradeoff: no slashing means no cryptoeconomic penalty for equivocation — Cardano's security rests on the cost of acquiring 51% of circulating ADA, not on bonded capital at risk. Reasonable people disagree on which is more robust under sustained adversarial pressure. But on decentralization and censorship resistance specifically, liquid stake is structurally better.

Recovery asymmetry — the part nobody talks about

This is the underrated half of the argument. Censorship and finality get the headlines, but recovery behavior is where the consensus families diverge most sharply.

Nakamoto degrades gracefully

40% adversarial → transactions confirm slower. 51% required before damage is irreversible. Recovery is automatic: when honest stake or hashrate returns, the longest chain wins. No coordination. No governance. No humans deciding which fork is canonical.

BFT stops

33% offline or byzantine → liveness failure. Recovery requires one of:

  • Protocol-level policy — Ethereum's inactivity leak slashes non-attesters until the active set drops under the 2/3 threshold and finality resumes. The protocol decides who counts as absent vs censoring vs partitioned.
  • Social consensus to restart — Cosmos chains have done this multiple times. After Terra/LUNA's collapse in May 2022, the chain was halted twice by validator coordination to apply security patches. Restart required humans deciding which fork was canonical.
  • A coordinated reboot — Solana validators on a Discord call, agreeing on which slot to resume from. This has happened seven times.

A chain that needs governance — algorithmic or human — to recover from a third of its validators going offline is, by construction, a federation with cryptography on top. Useful infrastructure. Not a neutral settlement layer.

The thesis

BFT chains are useful. They are fast. Many of them have produced genuinely innovative engineering. They are also excellent infrastructure for cooperative environments — consortia, app-chains, payment rails between known parties, anywhere the validator set is intentionally curated.

But "decentralized" in the sense Bitcoin meant it — no one to call when 33% of validators decide your transaction shouldn't settle — is a property only Nakamoto consensus has demonstrated at scale.

Solana made the tradeoff explicitly, prioritizing throughput and accepting concentrated, professionalized validation. Ethereum hedged with a hybrid that inherits BFT properties past finality. Cardano and Bitcoin didn't.

The question is not which family is "better" in the abstract. The question is what you are building. If you are building a consortium ledger, a high-throughput settlement layer for known parties, or a national payments rail, BFT may be exactly right. If you are building a neutral settlement layer for adversarial planetary-scale finance — one that must survive 33% of its validators being subpoenaed, sanctioned, or jailed — only Nakamoto consensus has the threshold profile and the recovery behavior to clear the bar.
